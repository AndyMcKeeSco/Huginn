# Knowledge Research

## Purpose
Answer a Learning Objective from **existing knowledge** — literature, market, competitor,
regulatory and domain sources — as the often-cheapest sufficiently reliable modality.

## When to use
- When the uncertainty may already be answered by existing sources.
- As a cheap first pass before primary research.

## When NOT to use
- When only primary observation can answer it (use `user-research` / `technical-investigation`).

## Inputs
- A Test's Learning Objective and evidence sought; source access.

## Definition of Ready
- A specific question and the kinds of sources that could answer it.

## Methodology selection
- **Desk/literature review**; **competitive & environmental scanning**; source-quality appraisal.

## Process
1. Identify credible sources; gather relevant material.
2. Capture each as an **artifact reference** with provenance (source, date, access).
3. Extract observations relevant to the Learning Objective.
4. Hand observations + provenance to `evidence-appraisal`.

## Structured output
Artifact references and extracted observations, ready for Evidence appraisal.

## Quality criteria
- Sources are credible and dated; provenance is complete; relevance is explicit.

## Definition of Done
- Enough appraised raw material for the Learning Steward to form Evidence.

## Failure modes
- Treating a source's claim as fact without appraisal.
- Missing provenance; cherry-picking confirming sources.

## Escalation / governance
- Raw material is not Evidence; only appraised observations with provenance become Evidence.
